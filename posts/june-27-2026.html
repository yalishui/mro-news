Market Updates and Growth Trends

IATA / Emerton Engine MRO Bottleneck Study — Major Report Released IATA

  • IATA in collaboration with Emerton released the report "Single Aisle Aircraft Engines MRO: Strategic Levers to Address Supply Chain Challenges" on June 24, 2026 (Madrid; Press Release 34)
  • Scope: Bottlenecks in MRO of latest-generation single-aisle engines — CFM LEAP and Pratt & Whitney Geared Turbofan (GTF)
  • Root causes identified: Engine durability issues, spare parts shortages, limited spare engine availability, constrained aftermarket access
  • Current state (March 2025 peak): 648 aircraft grounded — 28% of the GTF fleet — awaiting engine shop visits, spare engines, or parts
  • Single-aisle engine deliveries — 2024 actuals: 2,000 engines total (GTF: 800; LEAP: 1,200)
  • 2030–2040 stable delivery forecast: ~3,700 engines/year (GTF: 1,200; LEAP: 2,500)
  • Maintenance demand projections:
  • LEAP shop visits: ~600–800 (2025) → >5,000 by 2040
  • GTF shop visits: ~1,000 (2025) → >2,000 by 2040
  • Airline adaptations: Retaining older aircraft, extending leases, leasing additional aircraft, adjusting capacity
  • Quote (Willie Walsh, IATA Director General): "Engine MRO bottlenecks are disrupting airline operations. Without significant changes, this will only get worse as the fleet of latest-generation single-aisle aircraft grows."

USM / Asset Recovery Market Continues to Strengthen

  • AMTRA Aero Component Solutions (Tulsa, OK) acquired Airbus A330-200 airframe (MSN 529) from Cargo Aircraft Management (June 22, 2026)
  • Airframe slated for disassembly; harvested used serviceable material (USM) to be marketed through AMTRA's global sales network
  • Werner Aero acquired Airbus A321ceo (MSN 544) from FTAI Aviation (June 25, 2026)
  • Aircraft to undergo disassembly and asset recovery at Tarmac Aerosave in Tarbes, France
  • Market signal: A330 platform (both classic and neo) prominent across recent USM activity, reflecting sustained demand for A330 spare parts and growing teardown pipeline

Vallair Expands A330neo Base Maintenance Capability

  • Vallair obtained regulatory approval to perform base maintenance on Airbus A330neo aircraft at Châteauroux, France (June 25, 2026)
  • Approved variants: A330-800 and A330-900
  • Scope of approval: Scheduled heavy maintenance checks, structural inspections, modifications, related technical services
  • Vallair position: Expansion addresses growing A330neo MRO demand; widens competitive base maintenance options in Western Europe

Supply Chain Developments

IATA's 4-Stage Engine Aftermarket Liberalization Plan IATA

  • IATA + Emerton report outlines a structured industry reform path:

1. Increase engine parts availability — accelerate repair solution approvals, expand licensed production of critical components, expand access to USM from engine teardowns

2. Ensure fair access to the MRO market — remove barriers limiting independent MRO participation, support fair access to parts/repair info/tools; builds on the IATA-CFM agreement (signed 2018, renewed January 2026)

3. Secure long-term access to spare parts — embed long-term access and predictable spare parts pricing in aircraft/engine acquisition decisions; protections assignable to chosen MROs (including independents)

4. Adopt industry-wide best practices — OEMs (engine, airframe, component) should adopt transparent aftermarket principles, support customer choice and regulator-approved alternative parts/repairs

  • Walsh: "Resolving today's disruption is the immediate priority. But long-term resilience will depend on a more transparent, competitive, and collaborative aftermarket."

Major Challenges and Solutions

Challenge 1: Engine MRO Capacity Bottleneck Threatens Single-Aisle Growth

  • Risk: 648 aircraft (28% of GTF fleet) grounded at March 2025 peak; LEAP shop visits to grow ~7x by 2040; airlines forced to retain older aircraft, extend leases, add capacity through expensive short-term measures
  • IATA / Emerton solution: 4-stage framework (parts availability, fair MRO market access, long-term spare parts security, industry-wide best practices); build on renewed IATA-CFM agreement (Jan 2026)
  • Walsh: "Capacity alone will not be enough. Airlines need better access to spare parts, more approved repair options, fair access to MRO capacity, and greater competition in the aftermarket."

Challenge 2: Digital Fragmentation Across MRO Networks

  • Risk: Airlines/MROs juggling dozens of customer worksheets, manual paper processes, and inconsistent data capture across global station networks
  • FL Technics solution: Sensus MRO platform consolidates fragmented systems into a single program with automated work packs, TAT forecasting, intelligent inventory, and real-time load balancing
  • FEAM solution: Off-the-shelf / embedded AI to minimize investment risk while building customer portal and standardizing digital workflows across ~40 international locations

Challenge 3: Sustainability / Fuel Cost Pressure on Aging Fleets

  • Risk: Airlines delaying retirements (driven by OEM delivery delays) face compounding fuel-burn penalties; sustainability targets hard to meet
  • Solution examples:
  • Delta + VCT 737 finlets (1.2% fuel burn reduction validated; fleetwide rollout)
  • Industry push to adopt aerodynamic retrofits as a routine MRO activity

Challenge 4: Aftermarket / Spare Parts Access Concentration

  • Risk: OEM-controlled spare parts and licensed production create supply concentration; airlines dependent on a small number of approved MRO sources
  • IATA / industry solution: Expand USM channels, accelerate repair solution approvals, support regulator-approved alternative parts, embed fair-access provisions in procurement contracts

Challenge 5: Specialized MRO Talent and Capability Gaps (A330neo)

  • Risk: A330neo fleet growth outpacing certified base maintenance capacity; airlines face limited options for heavy checks, structural inspections, modifications
  • Solution: Vallair A330neo approval in Châteauroux adds competitive capacity for A330-800/-900 heavy maintenance; FEAM scaling pods/cohabitation model for mixed-fleet line maintenance (Atlas Air, GlobalX in Miami)

Challenge 6: Climate-Driven Demand for Aerial Firefighting

  • Risk: Intensifying wildfire seasons straining legacy C-130 / RJ-85 tanker fleets
  • Solution: Neptune Aviation + Commercial Jet A319 conversion in Dothan, AL — modernized, higher-capacity airtanker for the 2028 wildfire season
